# OCR correction and academic screening

## OCR correction

Corrections must remain reversible and source-faithful:

- Repair obvious character substitutions, broken words, hyphenation, spacing, and paragraph boundaries.
- Remove repeated interface chrome only when it is clearly not note content.
- Do not summarize, expand, translate, add citations, or complete an author's unfinished thought.
- Preserve uncertain source text or mark it `[无法确认]`.
- Treat extracted images and raw OCR as the record of truth; model-cleaned text is a convenience layer.

## Academic-depth rubric

Score each dimension from 0 to 5, then convert the sum to a percentage:

1. Conceptual depth: theories, definitions, mechanisms, or formal distinctions.
2. Evidence: data, experiments, cases, quotations, or traceable factual support.
3. Method: research design, analytic method, assumptions, limitations, or reproducibility.
4. Source grounding: identifiable authors, works, papers, institutions, or links.
5. Research usefulness: precise questions, non-obvious claims, productive disagreements, or connections worth investigating.

Suggested labels:

- 80–100: high academic value; prioritize for close research.
- 60–79: medium-high; useful with source verification.
- 40–59: mixed; mostly conceptual commentary or secondary explanation.
- 0–39: low; mainly personal reflection, promotion, or unsupported claims.

The score measures suitability for further research, not whether the author is correct. Never invent missing references. Clearly label proposed research questions as model suggestions.
